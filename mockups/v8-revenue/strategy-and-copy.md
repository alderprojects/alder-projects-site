# Alder Projects v8 — Revenue-Maximizing Homepage Concepts

Three concepts, each a different bet on what's actually limiting Smart Cart pull-through today: friction, trust, or differentiation. One image mockup per concept. Pick one to ship; modules are stealable across concepts regardless.

**Benchmarks anchoring this work:**
- $20 impulse-DTC homepages: 0.8–1.8% cold organic, 2–4% paid, 4–7% warm/branded.
- Free-tool homepages: 12–25% email capture, 3–8% paid conversion of tool-completers.
- Typical home/DIY SKU AOV: ~$32 with bundling, ~$22 without.

---

## A note on Concept C

The market strategist substituted a "This Week in Vermont Homes" weekly-editorial concept for the originally-proposed Photo-First, on the basis that photo-assisted reads aren't shipped — and promising an unshipped feature on the homepage is a credibility tax on a brand that trades on Vermont-restraint.

The copy agent stayed the course on Photo-First (which matches the existing "Coming next" teaser on the proposed mockup).

**Both versions are documented below.** The image-mockup pipeline renders the Photo-First version (since the proposed mockup already features it as a roadmap promise). If you'd rather see the weekly-editorial bet rendered instead, say the word — it's a one-line change in the script.

# Concept A — The Receipt
*Conversion Maximizer*

## Strategic thesis
Treat the homepage as a long-form sales page anchored to a single, math-driven promise: "the cart pays for itself by stopping one wrong purchase," made literal with a running tally of dollars Vermont homeowners didn't spend. One CTA, one price, one number, one project example — built for paid traffic and branded search to convert at the upper end of the $20-SKU range (target 3.5–5%).

## Optimizes for
Conversion over traffic. Warm and paid-search visitors. Audience archetype: the budget-anxious DIY-er who's already burned $200 at Aubuchon on the wrong thing once and remembers it. Deprioritizes cold SEO crawlers — they get served by the SEO landing-page network, not the homepage.

## Above-the-fold structure
Full-bleed split. Left 55%: dense single column — eyebrow / headline / sub / one CTA / micro-proofs strip. Right 45%: a static "receipt" graphic — a real-feeling Smart Cart preview for kitchen backsplash with Buy items and Skip items (strikethroughs), total "you didn't spend $X" at the bottom. No address input. No second CTA above the fold. The single number that has to be there: an average-savings figure ("$184 average skipped purchase per cart") placed directly under the CTA so it's the last thing read before the click.

## The hero promise
Tension: every Vermont project starts with a Home Depot run that includes one item you'll regret. Implicit fix: $19.99 buys you a list that flags it before you load the truck. Loss aversion, not aspiration — we're not selling a beautiful kitchen, we're selling not-getting-burned.

## Conversion levers below the fold
1. **Sample cart, full-fidelity.** Every Buy/Skip line, every dollar figure for the kitchen backsplash. Specificity → trust.
2. **The math, made stupid-simple.** Three-tile row: **$19.99** (one project, one fee) · **$184** (average skipped purchase) · **30 days** (refund, no form). Footnote linking to methodology on the savings figure.
3. **Three short testimonials with project names and towns.** "Saved me from a $240 faucet I'd have hated — Sarah, Stowe." Real names, real towns.
4. **Money-back guarantee block, oversized.** Not a footer line — a full section: "Don't save more than $19.99? Email us. Full refund, no questions, no form."
5. **Project grid (12–16 tiles).** SEO juice — every tile links to a project landing page. Below conversion zone so it doesn't compete.
6. **Email capture, single field, single promise.** "One Vermont project tip per week. No upsells."

## Risk reversal
Unconditional and frictionless: "If your Smart Cart doesn't save you at least $19.99, reply to your receipt email. We'll refund you. No form, no proof required."

## SEO play
Modest. Homepage ranks for "Alder Projects" and a few brand-adjacent terms; the 60+ SEO landing pages do the organic heavy lifting. Project grid section is the internal-linking hub. Schema: Product + AggregateRating + FAQ on the sample-cart section.

## Deliberately leaves out
Address-first input, dual hero CTAs, the "Coming next: photo-assisted reads" teaser, the four-pillar value-props row, editorial brand-storytelling tone, the seasonal strip.

## Sharpest risk
Feels transactional and lowers brand authority — Vermont homeowners are quietly skeptical and "9.2x ROI!" can read like a Facebook ad in a market that prefers understatement. Mitigation: tone of copy (dry, factual, Yankee-restrained) and design restraint (cream, one accent color, one CTA, no badges or stamps).

## Copy block

**Eyebrow:** SMART CART · VERMONT EDITION

**H1:** The shopping list for your *next* Vermont home project.

**Sub:** Tell us the project. We send back the buy-it list, the skip-it list, and the two or three things that are different here than in the box-store guide.

**Primary CTA:** Get the Smart Cart — $19.99
**Secondary CTA:** See a sample cart
**Hero proof line:** One skipped $40 cabinet pull pays for the cart twice.
**Trust strip:** Built in Montpelier · 30-day refund · No subscription · No account

**Sample cart card (right of headline):**
> KITCHEN BACKSPLASH · 4 sq ft
> - Buy: 1/8" notched trowel ($9)
> - Buy: pre-mixed mastic, not thinset ($22)
> - Skip: the spacer kit. Use nickels.
> - Worth a pro: the outlet relocation
> - Vermont note: ask the hardware store to cut tile. Most will.

**Section 2 — What's in the cart**
> Five things, every cart.
> 1. The buy list. With sizes, quantities, and the brand we'd actually pick.
> 2. The skip list. What the YouTube video told you to buy that you don't need.
> 3. The "wait until" list. Stuff that's cheaper in October.
> 4. The "this needs a licensed person" line. Drawn the way Vermont draws it.
> 5. One Efficiency Vermont rebate, if there's one to catch.

**Section 3 — Why $19.99**
> The math is the pitch.
> Most Vermont DIY projects have one $30–$80 purchase that's wrong for the job. Wrong adhesive. Wrong fastener. Wrong-grade lumber for an unheated mudroom. The cart's whole job is to catch that one. If it doesn't, ask for your money back. We'd rather you keep the $19.99 than pretend it earned its keep.

**Risk reversal block:**
> 30-day refund, no form, no "tell us why." Reply to the receipt with the word "refund" and we send the $19.99 back the same day. We're betting you'll keep it.

**Final CTA banner:**
> Tell us the project. We'll do the list.
> *Average turnaround on a weekday: 47 minutes.*
> [Start a Smart Cart — $19.99]

**Footer subscribe:**
> One Vermont project tip, every other Friday.
> What's worth doing this month, what to wait on, one rebate that's about to close.
> [Send me the list]

**Microcopy:**
- Form placeholder (project field): "kitchen backsplash, 1920s house, Burlington"
- Pricing badge: "$19.99 · one project · keep forever"
- Refund tooltip: "Reply 'refund.' That's the whole process."

# Concept B — The Two-Minute Read
*Free Diagnostic → Paid Cart Funnel*

## Strategic thesis
The hero is a free 3-question diagnostic that produces a personalized "Project Read" — and the Smart Cart is positioned as the upgrade for the specific project the visitor just told us about. The bet: Smart Cart's biggest enemy isn't price, it's "is this for my project?" — and qualifying the visitor before asking for $19.99 should lift conversion of the qualified pool 2–3x even if it shrinks the top of the funnel.

## Optimizes for
Conversion of cold-and-medium-warm traffic, plus email capture as a hard secondary KPI. Audience archetype: project-curious but pre-decision homeowners. Best for SEO traffic landing on the homepage from broad informational queries. Target: 18–25% diagnostic completion of homepage visitors, 22–30% email capture of completers, 5–8% paid Smart Cart conversion of completers.

## Above-the-fold structure
Centered, single-column, generous whitespace. Cream background. Headline above, then the diagnostic itself rendered as the hero — not a button leading to a quiz, the actual Question 1 of 3 sitting on the page with three to five large clickable answer chips. Progress dots showing 1-of-3 visible. Below the question: a small gray line — "Free. 30 seconds. No email required to see your result." Vermont imagery is incidental — a small framed photo of a Vermont farmhouse in the upper-right corner.

The three questions: (1) what's the project? (2) when are you doing it? (3) what's your budget zone? Q1 is the highest-information question and the only one strictly required.

## The hero promise
Tension: you're not sure if you should do this project now, do it yourself, or hire it out — and every blog post is generic. Implicit fix: tell us three things and we'll give you a Vermont-specific read on whether to act, when, and what to watch for. The Smart Cart is positioned as "the next step if the read says go" — not the hero offer.

## Conversion levers below the fold
1. **What you get from the read (3 examples).** Sample reads for kitchen / deck / winterization — three bullets each. Anchors expectation, prevents "is this just a quiz" bounce.
2. **Why the Smart Cart upgrade is worth it.** Direct comparison: free read tells you whether to do the project; Smart Cart tells you exactly what to buy and skip — with prices, brands, and the Vermont-specific gotchas. Anti-cannibalization framing.
3. **One full sample Smart Cart, openly visible.** Same as Concept A. Direct-buy escape hatch for visitors who skip the diagnostic.
4. **Project / town SEO grid.** Internal-linking role.
5. **Footer email capture: seasonal Vermont project tips** (different value prop from in-flow capture).

## Risk reversal
Two-layered. (1) The diagnostic itself — visitor invests 30 seconds and gets value before being asked for money, inverting the trust dynamic. (2) Same money-back guarantee as Concept A, stated more quietly because the qualified-buyer dynamic does most of the work.

## SEO play
Strongest of the three. The diagnostic generates a unique result URL per project type (e.g. /read/kitchen-refresh) — those become indexable, internally-linked content pages with real depth, each linking back to relevant town/project landing pages. Result pages are highly shareable, earning a long tail of natural backlinks. The homepage becomes a content hub, not a sales page. Schema: Quiz/HowTo on result pages, FAQ on homepage.

## Deliberately leaves out
The "Build a Smart Cart — $19.99" CTA above the fold (deliberately demoted to post-diagnostic). Address-first input. Heavy Vermont-cinematic imagery. Above-fold sample card. Four value-prop row.

## Sharpest risk
Cannibalization. If the free read is too generous, qualified visitors take the read, feel satisfied, and never upgrade. Mitigation: the free read tells you *whether* and *when*; the Smart Cart tells you *what to buy and skip*. Two products must be structurally non-overlapping.

## Copy block

**Eyebrow:** TWO-MINUTE PROJECT TRIAGE

**H1:** Three questions. We tell you what's *actually* worth doing.

**Sub:** Free triage for Vermont homeowners with a list of projects and not enough weekends. We'll rank them by cost, urgency, and what mud season is about to do to them.

**Primary CTA:** Start the triage
**Secondary CTA:** Skip to the Smart Cart
**Hero proof line:** Free. No email until you want the result emailed.

**Quiz card (centered, replaces hero image):**
> Question 1 of 3
> **What part of the house is bugging you most right now?**
> - Kitchen or bath
> - Basement, attic, or insulation
> - Roof, gutters, or siding
> - Outside: deck, walk, drive, yard
> - Honestly, I have a list
>
> Progress: ● ○ ○
> *Built on real Vermont permit and rebate data. Updated this week.*

**Section 2 — What you get back**
> Your one-page triage.
> - The project to do *first*. With the reason, not just the rank.
> - The one that can wait until next year without getting worse.
> - The one that's a pro job in Vermont even if it isn't elsewhere.
> - An estimated cash range, in 2026 dollars, for your county.
> - One rebate or tax credit you might be leaving on the table.

**Section 3 — Then, if you want, the cart**
> $19.99 turns the triage into a shopping list.
> The free result tells you *what* to do. The Smart Cart tells you *what to buy* — exact materials, exact quantities, what to skip, what to borrow instead of buy. One project, one cart, $19.99. You'll see it offered after the triage. You can also leave without it.

**Section 4 — Why we built the free part**
> Most Vermont project advice is written for somewhere else.
> The internet thinks you're in Atlanta. Your house is balloon-framed, the foundation is fieldstone, the basement floods in April, and the nearest big-box is 40 minutes. The triage is calibrated for that. It's free because the cart is the product, and we'd rather you only buy a cart for a project that's actually worth doing.

**Section 5 — What it isn't**
> Plain about what we're not.
> - Not a contractor directory. We won't sell your address to a roofer.
> - Not an inspection. We can't see your house.
> - Not financial advice. The numbers are ranges, not quotes.
> - Not a subscription. One project, one fee, done.

**Risk reversal block:**
> The triage is free and stays free. If you buy a Smart Cart and the list is wrong for your project, reply to the receipt and we refund the $19.99 the same day. No reason needed.

**Final CTA banner:**
> You have a list. We have a method.
> *Two minutes. Three questions. No account.*
> [Start the triage]

**Footer subscribe:**
> The Vermont project shortlist, monthly.
> One thing to do this month, one thing to skip, one rebate closing soon.
> [Subscribe]

**Microcopy:**
- Quiz back button: "← previous question"
- Result page header: "Your triage · prepared [date] · for [town]"
- Soft upsell button on result page: "Turn this into a Smart Cart — $19.99"
- Decline-the-cart link: "No thanks, just the triage"

# Concept C — Photo-First Vermont-Native
*A quieter way to start a project*

## Strategic thesis (copy agent's framing)
The hero is a photo-upload zone. Snap your kitchen, bath, basement, or back deck — receive an editorial-style "reading" of what's worth fixing, what to ignore, and what needs a second opinion. The Smart Cart becomes the natural upgrade for any "worth doing" item. The bet: Vermont homes are too idiosyncratic for forms; a photo grounds the conversation in *this* house, which raises perceived fit and conversion of qualified buyers. Most distinctive brand bet of the three.

## Strategist's flag (read this)
Photo-assisted reads aren't shipped yet. Putting the photo upload in the hero promises a feature that doesn't exist — and Alder's brand authority in this market depends on quiet credibility, not roadmap promises. **Recommended mitigation:** ship this homepage *only after* the photo flow is live (even a slow human-loop version), or render the upload zone as an explicit beta with a waitlist (kills above-the-fold conversion to Smart Cart but keeps the credibility intact).

## Optimizes for
Brand differentiation, email/waitlist capture, qualified-buyer conversion. Audience archetype: the homeowner with an old Vermont house who doesn't want to fill out a form to get generic advice. Worst for: cold paid traffic that hasn't seen the brand before (the photo upload is a high-trust ask).

## Above-the-fold structure
Cream background, editorial restraint. Eyebrow / H1 / sub stacked left or centered. Centered photo upload zone with dashed border, cloud-upload icon, "Drag a photo here, or take one now. JPG or HEIC. We don't store it after the reading." Below the upload zone: a sample reading card — "1880 farmhouse kitchen, Hardwick" with 3–4 bullet observations including one with a Smart Cart upgrade hook.

## The hero promise
Tension: every Vermont house is weirder than the form on every other home-advice site. Implicit fix: a photo lets us start where you actually are.

## Conversion levers below the fold
1. **What a reading looks like.** "Three lists, one page" — Worth doing / Worth ignoring / Worth a second opinion. Shown as a real example.
2. **Then, the cart.** $19.99 upgrade for any "worth doing" item. The reading is yours either way.
3. **What we look at, what we don't.** Honest list of what a photo can/can't tell us. Trust through limitation.
4. **Why a photo.** The Vermont-houses-are-weirder-than-forms argument.
5. **Project + town SEO grid** at the bottom — internal-linking unchanged.

## Risk reversal
The reading is free. Photo deleted after reading. We don't train on it. Money-back on Smart Carts unchanged.

## SEO play
Weakest of the three for direct organic. Photo-upload pages don't index well; the homepage relies on brand search and the project grid for traffic. Long-tail content is the published-with-permission readings (anonymous), which become a unique content type no competitor can easily replicate.

## Deliberately leaves out
Address input. Dual CTAs. Aggressive sales psychology. Static value-prop row. Most of the project grid above the fold.

## Sharpest risk
Promise overhang on an unshipped feature (see strategist's flag above).

## Copy block

**Eyebrow:** A QUIETER WAY TO START A PROJECT

**H1:** Take a picture of the *room.* We'll tell you what's worth fixing.

**Sub:** Snap your kitchen, bath, basement, or back deck. We send back what a careful Vermont neighbor would notice, in plain language, with the things you can ignore clearly marked ignore.

**Primary CTA:** Upload a photo
**Secondary CTA:** See an example reading
**Hero proof line:** We read the photo. Then a person checks it. Both, every time.

**Photo upload zone (centered, large):**
> Drag a photo here, or take one now.
> *JPG or HEIC. We don't store it after the reading.*

**Example caption (small, under upload zone):**
> Sample reading: 1880 farmhouse kitchen, Hardwick.
> - The cabinets are fine. Paint, don't replace.
> - The outlet next to the sink is pre-GFCI. Worth fixing.
> - The sag in the floor by the fridge is the joist, not the subfloor. Different project.
> - The backsplash is a $90 weekend. Smart Cart available.

**Section 2 — What a reading looks like**
> Three lists, one page.
> - **Worth doing.** With a why. Sometimes with a photo callout.
> - **Worth ignoring.** The thing that looks bad but isn't a problem.
> - **Worth a second opinion.** What a photo can't tell us, and who in Vermont to ask.

**Section 3 — Then, the cart**
> If something's worth doing, the cart is $19.99.
> A reading is free. If one of the worth-doing items is a project you'd actually take on, we can build the Smart Cart for it: the buy list, the skip list, the wait-until-fall list. One project, one cart. The reading is yours either way.

**Section 4 — What we look at**
> What the photo tells us, and what it doesn't.
> **Tells us:** finish age, obvious moisture, pre-code wiring, mismatched repairs, fixture era, cabinet bones, tile cracking patterns, ice-dam evidence on eaves.
> **Doesn't:** what's behind the wall, code in your specific town, anything load-bearing, mold versus stain. We say so when that's the answer.

**Section 5 — Why a photo**
> Because Vermont houses are weirder than forms.
> A 1790 cape, a 1972 split-level, and a 2019 ICF house don't share a checklist. A photo lets us start where you actually are. The rest of the internet wants you to pick a year and a style. We'd rather just look.

**Risk reversal block:**
> The reading is free. If you order a Smart Cart based on the reading and it doesn't match your project, reply to the receipt — we refund the $19.99 the same day. We keep the photo only long enough to write the reading, then it's gone.

**Final CTA banner:**
> Show us the room. We'll tell you what we see.
> *Free reading. About four minutes for the AI pass, an hour for the human one.*
> [Upload a photo]

**Footer subscribe:**
> A reading a month, on us.
> We pick one reader's photo each month, do the full reading, publish it anonymous. Want yours considered?
> [Send me a photo each month]

**Microcopy:**
- Upload-zone hover state: "Drop it. We'll take it from here."
- Privacy line under upload: "Photo deleted after reading. We don't train on it."
- Reading-page section labels: "Worth doing · Worth ignoring · Worth a second opinion"
- Cart upsell on reading page: "Build the Smart Cart for this one — $19.99"

# Strategist's alternative C — This Week in Vermont Homes

**Not rendered as an image mockup**, but documented for consideration as a substitute for Photo-First if you want to avoid the unshipped-feature credibility tax.

## Thesis
Make the homepage a living, weekly-refreshed seasonal artifact — May 9 is mud season + deck prep; October is winterization + pellet stoves. Smart Cart pull-through comes from season-aligned urgency ("you have 3 weekends left to seal the deck"), not from generic conversion psychology. Bet: the same product converts 2x better when the visitor believes the page was written *this week* for *this season*, and that timeliness earns disproportionate organic traffic and email subscriptions.

## Above-the-fold (sketch)
Top banner: thin date strip — "Week of May 9 — Mud Season, Week 4." Two-column hero. Left 60%: this week's headline ("3 deck repairs to do before Memorial Day") + 2–3 sentence editorial lede. Right 40%: card titled "This week's Smart Cart: Deck Repair — $19.99" with Buy/Skip preview and CTA. Single proof: "Updated weekly. Read by 8,400 Vermont homeowners."

## Why it might be the right C
- Strongest organic SEO compounding (50+ dated content pages over 12 months).
- Email becomes a real distribution channel.
- Most genuinely differentiated in a sea of evergreen DTC homepages.

## Why it might not be
**Operational.** Concept dies if the weekly update slips. A homepage dated "Week of May 9" still up on May 23 is worse than no dated content at all. Requires either real editorial cadence (~4 hours/week) or a templatized workflow. Honest question before committing: can this team ship weekly for 26 weeks straight? If the answer is "probably 18 of 26," pick A or B instead.

---

# Recommendation

If revenue this quarter is the goal, build **Concept A** — lowest risk, highest conversion floor, receipt aesthetic genuinely differentiated.

If you have engineering bandwidth and want a homepage that compounds, build **Concept B** — the diagnostic is the only one that meaningfully grows the email list, and email is the long-term asset for a $19.99 SKU.

**Concept C (Photo-First):** ship only after the photo flow is live, even in human-loop form. Otherwise it's a credibility risk dressed as a homepage.

**Strategist's C-substitute (This Week):** the most distinctive bet, but a content-operations commitment, not a design project.

- The full-fidelity sample Smart Cart (all three concepts)
- The unconditional money-back guarantee block (all three)
- The seasonal-filtered project grid from the strategist's C alternative
